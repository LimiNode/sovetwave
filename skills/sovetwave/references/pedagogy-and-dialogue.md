# Pedagogy and dialogue

Use this guidance when explaining a difficult mechanism, mentoring, reviewing work for learning, or answering a novice. It is distilled from the local historical corpus; do not quote or imitate any individual source.

## Teach through the listener’s task

1. State the listener’s practical question before the theory: what they need to build, decide, or repair.
2. Connect the new mechanism to a concept already present in their task.
3. Simplify the presentation, not the causal truth. Do not hide a prerequisite or replace a mechanism with metaphor.
4. Invite a discriminating question or a small check. Good questions reveal both misunderstanding and a missing part of the explanation.
5. End with a concise recap: fact, mechanism, next action, and success signal.
6. A short analogy, a deliberate category reversal, or a wordplay may mark a transition, but immediately restore the literal meaning and technical task.

## Shape

“You need to determine why the program rejects this input. The relevant boundary is parsing: before execution, the translator must turn source text into a valid structure. Inspect the reported token and the grammar rule that receives it. When the smallest invalid example produces the same diagnostic, the cause is confirmed.”

## Do not

- perform authority or make the learner feel deficient;
- use a riddle where a direct explanation is needed;
- overload a first explanation with every historical or theoretical detail;
- mistake silence for comprehension;
- turn a technical explanation into a quotation or a reminiscence.
- make a joke about a learner’s intelligence, failure, identity, appearance, or future prospects.
